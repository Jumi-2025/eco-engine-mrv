# UNICEF Venture Fund application notes

Source checked 18 September 2026: https://www.unicefventurefund.org/apply-funding

The Fund lists private for-profit registration in a UNICEF programme country, an existing prototype with promising pilot results, frontier technology, measurable public real-time data, and potential positive impact on vulnerable children. It supports open-source solutions or a stated commitment to open-source the funded solution. Check the specific call before submission.

## Accurate prototype description

ECO-ENGINE is an industrial MRV prototype demonstrating evidence intake, human approval, deterministic emissions calculations and an audit history. Its current extraction workflow uses simulated suggestions based on fixtures rather than a live AI extraction model. Reviewer decisions and regulator report packaging are planned work. We are seeking validation through real-data pilots; synthetic demonstrations are not evidence of customer or child-health outcomes.

## Fit that needs evidence

Industrial emissions reporting can support climate accountability, but that does not by itself demonstrate direct benefit to vulnerable children. Explain the actual users and decisions, the route from emissions evidence to mitigation action, and how a qualified partner would measure outcomes relevant to children. Do not describe greenhouse-gas totals as measurements of local air-pollution exposure or child health.

Document any real pilot results, operator commitments and implementation partners you actually have. Distinguish proposed measures from achieved results. A public demonstration database is not a suitable impact-monitoring dataset.

## Licensing

The inherited package manifest says ISC, but no signed-off standalone license was supplied. Confirm ownership of the source and the intended open-source license, add its complete license text, and describe that license accurately in the application. If not yet released under an open-source license, use the Fund's stated willingness-to-open-source route and give a concrete timeline rather than claiming completion.

## Do not overstate

Do not claim UNICEF endorsement, verified regulatory compliance, production AI, live sensor integrations, paying customers, emissions reductions or improved child-health outcomes unless supported by evidence.
